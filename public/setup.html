♞ Chess Glasses setup

Look at a chess board through Meta glasses, take a photo, and hear the best move. Setup takes about 5 minutes on your iPhone.

1 Choose how photos are read

Board reading uses Google's Gemini AI. Pick one:

Free shared key nothing to set up

Use this site's key. You get a number of free scans each day.

✓ You're using the free shared key.

Your own free key unlimited

Sign in at aistudio.google.com/apikey, tap Create API key, copy it, and paste it here. It's free and takes about two minutes.

Your key is saved only on this device and in your Shortcut. It's sent with each photo so Google can read the board, and this server never stores it.

2 Which side are you playing?

Change this at the start of each game. It tells the app which way the board faces and when it's your move.

3 Add the iPhone Shortcut

Tap below on your iPhone. When Shortcuts asks, paste your chess link, and your Gemini key if you added one (otherwise leave it empty). Both are shown below.

Or build the Shortcut yourself
  1. Open Shortcuts, tap +, and name it Chess move.
  2. Add Get Latest Photos (1 photo, Include Screenshots off).
  3. Add Convert Image → JPEG.
  4. Add Resize Image → width 1400.
  5. Add Get Contents of URL: paste your chess link. Expand it: Method POST. Only if you added your own key: under Headers, add a header named x-gemini-key with your key as its value. Set Request Body to File and choose Resized Image.
  6. Add Get Dictionary Value → key say in Contents of URL.
  7. Add Speak Text → Dictionary Value.

4 Make it hands-free

  1. In the Meta AI app, turn on automatic import of photos from your glasses to the Photos app.
  2. On your iPhone: Settings → Accessibility → Touch → Back Tap → Double Tap → Chess move. Or use the Action Button, or say “Hey Siri, chess move”.

During a game, when it's your move: say “Hey Meta, take a photo” while looking at the board. Wait a few seconds for it to import, then double-tap the back of your phone. You'll hear something like “They played Nf6. Knight g1 to f3”. Take a photo after your own move too, so the app keeps track of the game.

5 Try it without a board

Open the test board on a computer, photograph its screen, and run the Shortcut. The page tells you whether the board was read correctly.

You can also follow your game on the live board page.
